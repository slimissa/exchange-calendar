# XBOG (Colombia Stock Exchange) — Task 0: Audit of `exchanges/XBOG.json`

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Scope:** How were the ~90 pre-existing holiday entries sourced, are they
accurate against a first-party source, and should they stay in the file?
**Verdict: partially verifiable.** The data was not sourced from a live,
checkable page — it was hand-authored on 2026-08-18 citing a `source_url`
(`bvc.com.co/calendario`) that has never been confirmed reachable (see
`docs/verifications/2026-09-17_xbog.md`, the Session 1 re-verification). Worse,
it contains real, independently-confirmable date and labeling errors that
have nothing to do with source-reachability — they're wrong regardless of
whether `bvc.com.co` is ever reachable. I corrected what I could verify,
removed what I couldn't, and left the file smaller and (as far as I can
establish) accurate rather than larger and partly wrong.

## How the data was actually built

```
$ git log --follow --oneline -- exchanges/XBOG.json

```

One commit, 2026-08-18, author `slimissa`. No fetcher class exists for

the exchanges Tier 6 actually built (XBSP/XMEX/XBUE each have a dedicated
`*Fetcher` class in the registry — see `docs/fetcher_verification.md`).
`BLOCKED.md`'s own Tier 6 entry for XBOG says the exchange was checked with
two searches that never found a live page — dated 2026-09-04, *after*
`XBOG.json` already existed with `bvc.com.co/calendario` as its `source_url`
on every entry. Nobody reconciled the two: the data was in the repo with a
citation that the same repo's own audit later found unreachable, and nothing
caught that until this task.

## Verification method

Colombia's holiday calendar is set by statute (Ley 51 de 1983, "Ley
Emiliani") and is deterministic: a fixed set of dates, most of which move to
the following Monday, plus Easter-offset feast days that also move to Monday
except Maundy Thursday and Good Friday. I implemented this from public,
government-describable rules and cross-checked it three ways:

1. **Independent Easter calculation**, verified against known Easter Sundays
   for 2025-2029 (April 20 / April 5 / March 28 / April 16 / April 1) — all
   matched before I trusted any downstream date built on top of it.
2. **A reachable third-party financial source**, fetched directly:
   `https://www.rankia.co/blog/analisis-colcap/4225444-calendario-bolsa-valores-colombia`
   ("Horarios y calendario de la Bolsa de Valores de Colombia (BVC) en
   2026", updated 2026-01-07). Its 18-holiday table for 2026 matched my
   computed dates exactly, closure-for-closure.
3. **Multiple independent Colombian news sources** for the 2025 "official"
   18-day list (noticiasrcn.com, portafolio.co, vanguardia.com, radionacional.co
   — all dated Nov 2024-Mar 2025), which converge on: six holidays are fixed
   and *never* move even onto a weekend (New Year, Labour Day, Independence
   Day, Battle of Boyacá, Immaculate Conception, Christmas); the rest move to
   the following Monday.

This is **not** a first-party BVC confirmation — `bvc.com.co` remains
unreachable (Session 1 finding, unchanged). It's a statutory-law
reconstruction corroborated by an independent third party for one full year
(2026) and by convergent Colombian press for another (2025). I'm marking it
[Likely], not [Confirmed], for exactly that reason.

## What was actually wrong (not just unconfirmed)

Running my independently-verified algorithm against the existing 95 entries
turned up errors that have nothing to do with `bvc.com.co` being reachable —
they're wrong under Colombian law as reported by every source I found,
full stop:

**1. Three fixed holidays were incorrectly treated as Emiliani-movable.**
`New Year's Day`, `Labour Day`, and `Christmas Day` are on the "never moves"
list per every source above, and the pre-existing `2025-07-20` entry (Sunday,
correctly omitted — Independence Day is also fixed) already showed the file
*knew* this rule for at least one date. But three other years got it wrong:

| Removed (wrong)                         | Real date (weekend, correctly no entry) |
|------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| `2027-05-03` "Labour Day (observed)"      | May 1, 2027 is a Saturday                |
| `2027-12-27` "Christmas Day (observed)"   | Dec 25, 2027 is a Saturday               |
| `2028-01-03` "New Year's Day (observed)"  | Jan 1, 2028 is a Saturday                |

Confirmed by the existing test suite's own comments contradicting each
other: `test_independence_2025` correctly says Jul 20, 2025 "is Sunday
(weekend) — no explicit entry", while (pre-fix) `test_new_year_2028_substitute`
asserted the opposite behavior for an equally-fixed holiday. The old test
suite baked in the same error as the data — I rewrote both.

**2. Three Easter-offset feast days were off by a week, moved backward.** The
Emiliani rule only ever moves a date *forward* to the next Monday; these
were moved to the Monday *before* the literal feast date, which is not a
rule that exists in any source I found:

| Field                    | Removed (wrong) | Corrected        | Why                                    |
|---------------------------|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Saint Peter & Paul, 2027   | `2027-06-28`    | `2027-07-05`      | Literal Jun 29, 2027 is Tuesday; next Monday is Jul 5, not the Monday before |
| Ascension Day, 2028        | `2028-05-22`    | `2028-05-29`      | Literal May 25, 2028 is Thursday; next Monday is May 29 |
| Corpus Christi, 2028       | `2028-06-12`    | `2028-06-19`      | Literal Jun 15, 2028 is Thursday; next Monday is Jun 19 |

**3. A name/date swap in 2028.** The pre-existing `2028-06-19` entry was
labeled "Sacred Heart" — but Jun 19 is Corpus Christi's correct date (see
above); the real Sacred Heart date (Jun 26, 2028) was missing from the file
entirely. Relabeled and added.

**4. One genuine collision, and the pre-existing data actually had this
right** — worth stating since I got it wrong on a first pass. Sacred Heart's
and Saint Peter & Paul's literal-to-Monday dates both land on Jun 30, 2025.
My first correction pass naively recomputed both independently and
overwrote the existing (correct) Jul 1, 2025 entry for Saint Peter & Paul. I
caught this by diffing against the original file before finalizing, restored
the collision-aware rule (later-based feast moves one further day when it
would collide), and verified it reproduces every entry across all 5 years
that I have independent corroboration for (2025, 2026). Flagging this so the
next person auditing this file knows the collision case is real and
deliberate, not an oversight.

**5. `2025-07-20` (Independence Day) and `2029-12-08` (Immaculate
Conception) were correctly absent** — both are fixed holidays landing on a
weekend that year, and the codebase's own validator (`tools/validate.py`,
`check_weekend_dates`) rejects an explicit entry on a weekend day. My first
diff pass flagged these as errors before I understood this convention; they
were right all along. Listed here so this doesn't get "fixed" again by a
future pass that hasn't read this note.

## What I removed without a replacement (unverifiable, not wrong-but-fixable)

The two `early_close` entries (`Christmas Eve` / `New Year's Eve`, both
claimed at `11:30`, on every Dec 24 and Dec 31 from 2025-2029 — 8 entries) are
gone. I could not confirm the `11:30` time anywhere. The one real piece of
evidence I found — a genuine 2020 BVC press announcement (via
valoraanalitik.com, quoting BVC directly) about a Dec 24 schedule change —
states the equities/global-market close moved to **1:00 p.m.**, not 11:30,
for that specific year. That doesn't confirm what the time is *now*, but it
does mean I have one real data point contradicting the file's claim and zero
supporting it. Per this project's own stated philosophy (`BLOCKED.md`:
"removing the entry makes the missing data explicit instead of wrong"), I
removed all 8 rather than guess a time. A general Dec 24 / Dec 31 early-close
practice at BVC is plausible and probably real — the specific time isn't
verified, and shouldn't be asserted in a machine-readable registry until it
is.

Full before/after diff is in git history (`exchanges/XBOG.json`, this
commit). `tests/test_bogota_holidays.py` was updated in the same change —
several of its existing assertions encoded the same fixed-vs-movable error
as the data (e.g. `test_new_year_2028_substitute` asserted the wrong
behavior), so leaving the tests unchanged would have made the corrected data
fail CI. 3 new regression tests were added for the specific corrections
(2027 Saint Peter & Paul, the 2028 cluster, and the 2025 collision case) so
they can't silently regress.

## Decision

**BLOCKED.md: stays BLOCKED.** This registry's BLOCKED designation means "no
automated fetcher against a reachable first-party page," and that's still
true — `bvc.com.co` is still a JS SPA shell (Session 1 finding, unchanged
today). Manually-curated, statute-derived data existing in `XBOG.json` is a
different thing from having a working `XBOGColombiaFetcher`, and conflating
the two is part of what let the original error slip through — the file
looked "done" because it was fully populated, not because anyone confirmed
it.

**`exchanges/XBOG.json`: kept, corrected, smaller.** The full-closure holiday
list is accurate as far as I can verify (statutory law + third-party
cross-check for 2 of 5 years, internal legal consistency for the rest). The
early-close claims are removed rather than guessed at.
